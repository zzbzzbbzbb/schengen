# Review Process (jope-review-process)

## When to trigger

- You are deciding between a regular submission and an open special collection
- You need realistic expectations about JOPE's desk screen, timelines, and acceptance rate
- You want to know who handles the paper and how the decision is made
- You are weighing a preprint (GLO or IZA discussion paper) before submission

## How JOPE review works

JOPE is published by **Springer** in cooperation with **POP at UNU-MERIT** and the **Global Labor
Organization (GLO)**; it was founded in 1987 by **Klaus F. Zimmermann**, who remains **Editor-in-Chief**.
**Madeline Zavodny** is Managing Editor; the editorial office sits at POP at UNU-MERIT in Maastricht
(JOPE-Office@glabor.org). A board of **Editors** handles papers by field (the 2025 list includes Ainoa
Aparicio Fenoll, Xi Chen, Shuaizhang Feng, Alfonso Flores-Lagunes, Oded Galor, Astghik Mavisakalyan,
Terra McKinnish, Milena Nikolova, Gregory Ponthiere, and Kompal Sinha), supported by Associate Editors.
Submission is via **Editorial Manager** (editorialmanager.com/jope).

**Desk screen.** JOPE has run a **desk-rejection policy since 2013** to give authors an early signal.
Editor-in-Chief reports put the acceptance rate at **9.5% (2017)**, **7% (2018)**, **4.9% (2019)**,
**5.4% (2020)**, and **7% (2021)**, with submissions rising by roughly a third and then by over 40% in
2020. Average time to first decision was **30 days (2019)**, **24 days (2020)**, and **23 days (2021)**;
the Springer journal page currently reports a **median of about 7 days** from submission to first
decision, a figure that includes desk rejections.

**Review model.** Whether JOPE review is single- or double-anonymized was **not verified** in the latest
refresh; Springer economics journals commonly use single-anonymized review. Follow the "Peer review"
section of the live submission guidelines and do not anonymize unless it says to.

**Fees and access.** No submission fee was located (Springer journals generally do not charge one).
The journal is **hybrid open access**: an open-access charge applies only if the author chooses Open
Choice after acceptance, and many institutions have Springer transformative agreements that cover it.

## Routes

### Regular submission
The default. A field editor is assigned after the desk screen; external referees; standard R&R rounds
via Editorial Manager.

### Special collections
JOPE continuously invites submissions to **special collections**, and the journal states that editors
treat those submissions with **particular interest and speedy handling**. Collections open at the latest
refresh included **abortions; COVID-19 and diseases; lifecycle fertility models; sexual and domestic
violence; sexuality including LGBT issues; and statistics and measurement of population economics**.
Check the current list on the journal page; collections open and close.

### Preprints and the GLO ecosystem
Springer's general policy permits preprints. GLO runs a discussion-paper series (**GLO Discussion
Papers**), and IZA Discussion Papers are common in the field; posting one is compatible with
submission. The **GLO-JOPE Global Conference** (held online in early December in 2023 and 2024)
presents journal highlights and awards the **Kuznets Prize** for the best article published in the
journal (annual since 2014; recent winners are listed in `../../resources/exemplars/library.md`).

## Choosing a route

- Fits an open collection and is ready → **collection submission** (name it in the cover letter and the
  Editorial Manager article type).
- Standard population-economics contribution → **regular submission**.
- Not yet polished but you want visibility → GLO/IZA discussion paper first; submit when the 150-word
  abstract and framing are desk-proof.

## Route-choice decision table

| Your situation                                                    | Route                 | Why                                                     |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| Abortion-access, domestic-violence, or LGBT-economics paper         | Special collection    | Named collection; faster handling signaled by the journal |
| Fertility, migration, ageing paper with a reform-based design       | Regular               | Core scope; field editor assignment                     |
| Measurement or data paper on population statistics                  | Collection (statistics & measurement) | Named collection                      |
| Labor paper with a demographic sample but no demographic mechanism  | Re-route              | Desk-screen risk; see jope-topic-selection              |

## Worked routing example (illustrative)

Hypothetical: a team has a difference-in-differences study of a state-level abortion restriction and
births, with a 240-word abstract.

- **Right call:** cut the abstract to 150 non-technical words, name the abortions collection in the cover
  letter, and select it in Editorial Manager; expect a first signal within weeks.
- **Wrong call:** submitting with the technical abstract and no collection — the desk screen is fast and
  the abstract is the first thing read.

## Calibration anchors (hedged)

- Selectivity is high: roughly 5–7% acceptance in recent EiC reports; the desk screen is where most
  rejections happen.
- First decisions are fast — weeks, not months — so a desk rejection costs little time but a weak
  abstract costs the submission.
- Rejected papers are generally not reconsidered unless the decision letter invites it (verify the
  current policy wording).

## Anti-patterns

- Submitting with an abstract over 150 words or full of estimator names
- Ignoring an open collection that fits the paper
- Anonymizing (or not) without reading the live peer-review section
- Assuming a submission fee or an APC applies at submission

## How to use this library

Pick the row whose **method × question** is closest to yours, then study how that paper makes an
economic force and a demographic margin speak to each other — the JOPE bar (see
[`../../skills/jope-topic-selection/jope-topic-selection.md`](../../skills/jope-topic-selection/jope-topic-selection.md),
[`../../skills/jope-identification-strategy/jope-identification-strategy.md`](../../skills/jope-identification-strategy/jope-identification-strategy.md), and
[`../../skills/jope-contribution-framing/jope-contribution-framing.md`](../../skills/jope-contribution-framing/jope-contribution-framing.md)). For
each, ask the self-check question before claiming your paper is "JOPE-shaped."

## Kuznets Prize winners (the journal's own signal of what it values)

The Kuznets Prize is awarded for the best article published in the journal. Recent winners, all
verified against the journal's announcements:

- **Hazarika, Jha & Sarangi, "Ancestral ecological endowments and missing women," JOPE 2019, 32(4):1101–1123** (2020 prize).
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** a long-run, historical-roots question — how ancestral ecological
    endowments relate to present-day missing women; the demographic outcome is the point, the economic
    mechanism is the explanation (design positioning from the title and prize announcement; read the
    paper for the identifying variation).
  - **Self-check:** does your historical variation identify a demographic mechanism, not only a correlation?

- **Qiu, Chen & Shi, "Impacts of social and economic factors on the transmission of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) in China," JOPE 2020, 33(4):1127–1172** (2021 prize).
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** a fast, policy-relevant paper on a societal challenge the journal
    explicitly lists (diseases), with social and economic factors as the transmission channels — the
    "COVID-19 and diseases" collection in action.
  - **Self-check:** if your paper is timely, is the population-economics mechanism still explicit?

- **Bonacini, Gallo & Scicchitano, "Working from home and income inequality: risks of a 'new normal' with COVID-19," JOPE 2021, 34(1):303–360** (2022 prize).
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** income inequality under a shock, through who can work from home — the
    distributional strand of the journal's scope.
  - **Self-check:** does your distributional result say which population groups gain and lose?

- **Rastogi & Sharma, "Unwanted daughters: the unintended consequences of a ban on sex-selective abortions on the educational attainment of women," JOPE 2022, 35:1473–1516** (2023 prize).
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** an abortion policy (a named JOPE collection topic) with an unintended
    consequence for women's educational attainment — a demographic policy with an economic outcome,
    exactly the economics-demography link `jope-contribution-framing` asks for. If your own design is a
    policy-reform DID, pair it with [`../code/stata/03_did_modern.do`](../code/stata/03_did_modern.do).
  - **Self-check:** does your policy evaluation report the *unintended* demographic margin, not only the targeted one?

- **Zhang & Breunig, "Female breadwinning and domestic abuse: evidence from Australia," JOPE 2023, 36:2925–2965** (2024 prize).
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** household bargaining and gender norms (sexual and domestic violence is a
    named collection); the relative-income margin inside the couple is the economic force, abuse the
    outcome.
  - **Self-check:** is the within-couple economic variable credibly separated from selection into couples?

- **Eibich & Zai, "Are the grandparents alright? The health consequences of grandparental childcare provision," JOPE 2024, 37, article 71** (2025 prize).
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** ageing, intergenerational time transfers, and health — the macro-ageing
    and health-care strands of the scope, at the micro level.
  - **Self-check:** does your ageing paper connect the older generation's behavior to the younger
    generation's constraints, or vice versa?

## By method (older canonical JOPE papers)

### Cross-country panel on institutions and fertility

- **Adserà, "Changing fertility rates in developed countries. The impact of labor market institutions," JOPE 2004, 17(1):17–43.**
  Verified: SpringerLink DOI `10.1007/s00148-003-0166-x`.
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** turns the reversal of the fertility–female-employment correlation across
    OECD countries into a labor-market-institutions story (unemployment, contract stability) — the
    macro-level economics-demography link.
  - **Self-check:** is your cross-country variation tied to a named institutional mechanism?

- **Ahn & Mira, "A note on the changing relationship between fertility and female employment rates in developed countries," JOPE 2002, 15(4):667–682.**
  Verified: SpringerLink DOI `10.1007/s001480100078`.
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** a short, sharp descriptive-plus-interpretation note that reframed a
    stylized fact; shows that JOPE publishes concise contributions when the demographic fact is first-order.
  - **Self-check:** if your paper is short, does it change how the field reads a fact?

### Household micro model of childcare, participation, and fertility

- **Del Boca, "The effect of child care and part time opportunities on participation and fertility decisions in Italy," JOPE 2002, 15(3):549–573.**
  Verified: SpringerLink DOI `10.1007/s001480100089`; RePEc `spr:jopoec:v:15:y:2002:i:3:p:549-573`.
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** joint modeling of labor-force participation and fertility with childcare
    availability and part-time opportunities as the economic constraints — the micro household-behavior
    strand of the scope.
  - **Self-check:** are participation and fertility treated as joint decisions where your data allow?

### Migration: location choice and networks

- **Bauer, Epstein & Gang, "Enclaves, language, and the location choice of migrants," JOPE 2005, 18(4):649–662.**
  Verified: SpringerLink DOI `10.1007/s00148-005-0009-z`; RePEc `spr:jopoec:v:18:y:2005:i:4:p:649-662`.
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** migrant location choice as a function of language proficiency and
    network size — a migration paper where the demographic composition (enclaves) is the mechanism.
  - **Self-check:** does your migration paper say what selection or network channel drives the result?

### Savings, social security, and fertility (theory + time series)

- **Cigno & Rosati, "The effects of financial markets and social security on saving and fertility behaviour in Italy," JOPE 1992, 5:319–341.**
  Verified: SpringerLink DOI `10.1007/BF00163064`.
  - **Why it is an exemplar:** derives saving and fertility implications from self-interest vs altruism
    models, then estimates them — the macro strand (savings and pensions, social security) with
    fertility endogenous.
  - **Self-check:** if your paper is theory-led, does it deliver a testable demographic implication and test it?
